# Listen, then reach

This skill judges and prepares; the owner contacts people.

## Listen (before building, and again whenever reach is the question)

If a research skill is installed (for example customer-research or last30days), hand the searching to it and judge what comes back; first check whether it sends the idea to third-party services, and let the owner decide. Otherwise search public sources by hand: where people talk about this problem, the words they use, the related queries search engines suggest. Three rules either way:

- **Grade every finding by what the source is.** High: several independent sources agree and record what people actually did (switched, paid, built a workaround). Medium: one source of recorded behaviour, or people describing their own past behaviour. Low: stated interest or opinion. Being prompted by a question lowers a source's weight but does not void it, and being unprompted does not make it reliable; a count of sources alone never makes it high. Look for evidence against the idea too.
- **Say versus do.** What people say is a lead; what they do (switch, pay, build a workaround, search) is evidence.
- **Name what you could not see.** Sources behind a login or a limit, and any source that failed, are reported as unseen, not as quiet. When the evidence is thin, say so.

## Reach (before promoting, or live with few or no visitors)

1. **Check the numbers.** Are visits real (not your own previews, not bots)? Where did the real ones come from? With no visits yet, go to step 2.
2. **Readiness.** Before public promotion (posts, launches, ads), try the core task on the public address, logged out, at phone width; say what you tried and what happened, and what you could not reach is unverified. Recommend that promotion wait until known blockers to the core task are fixed; a requested post is still drafted, marked not to send until then. A few testers, told what does not work yet, can be invited earlier. If user value is still uncertain, run a small reach test and use the result to update the hypothesis, not as a definitive answer about demand.
3. **Listen** as above, if it has not been done.
4. **Pick one.** One group of people you can reach, one sentence for them, one channel. When that group mostly finds things by search, check how they would find the product today and name a search or launch skill for the detail (Run step 1).
5. **Test small.** Write the success signal first (one number or behaviour) and the time to look. Prepare the post or message for the owner to send. Hand the test over in the project's existing record (issue, task, note): who reads, which signal, where, and the next check each reading points to, not a root cause or a feature to build. Until the post is out, write the review date as post date + N days, not a calendar date. Write the entry only when the owner's existing authorisation covers editing that file; otherwise give it paste-ready and ask whether to save it. "Remind me" alone does not cover it.
6. **Follow-up is scheduled** only when a reminder that outlives this session exists, or an existing recurring review covers it (name its record). Otherwise write "reminder not set" and who has to remember. A name in the record is a proposal until that person accepts.
7. **Read the result.** People acting (visiting, returning, asking, paying) is strong evidence; likes and replies are weak. Decide: continue, change group, message or channel, or stop. With few visits the result picks the next check; it does not settle demand either way.
