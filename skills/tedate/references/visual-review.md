# Visual review

For a page that looks wrong or misses the quality the owner wants, including after a design skill has run. The aim is to turn "it just looks off" into work a specialist can do and a person can judge.

## Steps

1. **Look at the real thing.** Real content, the key screen sizes, the states that change what shows (other dates, empty or long content), and the interactions that matter (scroll, expand, language switch, motion, responsive behaviour). Screenshots are part of the evidence; operate the page for the rest. State the quality wanted and the accepted direction, if any, and say what you took as the baseline (see `decisions.md`). Judge what you can see, and mark the sizes and states you have not seen as unverified; a claim that spans sizes needs screenshots at those sizes. If you cannot render the page at all, say the diagnosis is provisional and get screenshots (ask the owner, or take them yourself) before naming problems, and recommend a redesign only after the page has been seen.
2. **Name the two or three things that most affect the overall impression**, each with where it shows on the page. Give each a check that can be judged on the real page: measure what can be measured ("the primary action and key facts are visible without expanding", "repeated cards for one item take no more than half of the first screen"), otherwise a side-by-side comparison; say at which screen widths (include a phone width when mobile use is in scope). Carry the diagnosis yourself; the owner needs no design vocabulary.
3. **Classify each one**; several can hold at once.
   - Direction: the page does not express how the product should feel.
   - Information organisation: repeated content, wrong order for the task, no clear centre.
   - Continuity: parts or pages do not read as one voice (type, density, spacing, colour, components).
   - Execution: the chosen direction was carried out poorly.
4. **Offer the way forward.** End the diagnosis with the ways to change the page, offered as in Run step 5 (A, B, C, one marked recommended, each with what it changes and who does it); the pick becomes the task below. One clear fix is given alone.
5. **Hand over a comparable task.** Name the design skill that takes it (Run step 1). Then: what to keep (say which information must stay visible), what to change, why it should help, and which real page to judge it on. When a task lowers the weight of one element, it also names that element's siblings (the other half of a pair, the rest of a list), says whether they change with it, and says what the lowered element must still read as (quieter, not unavailable). Until it is rechecked on the same content, screen widths and expanded state, the change stays **awaiting verification**. A task names the cause and the first change: "the first screen gives every block similar weight, so the name, status and secondary text compete; build one visual centre first". Wishes such as "more premium" go back to step 2. With the direction open, ask for a few visibly different explorations and recommend one with the reason; with it settled, ask for faithful execution and no re-design each round.
6. **Check the result** on the same real page. Every problem named in step 2 gets a status: improved, unchanged or worse. Each element whose weight was lowered also gets a look: it still reads as available and matches its siblings. Compare the text on the page before and after; any text that is gone or changed in meaning, even if hidden by a condition, is reported; if the owner had not authorised it, the original stays until they accept the proposed wording. When a change brings no improvement, first test whether the diagnosis was wrong; before another attempt, state the new judgement and what you expect to improve. When the causes cannot be told apart, or every named problem has improved and the owner still finds the page wrong, propose one bounded comparison and stop tweaking. In the second case the status is **awaiting the owner's view**: recheck the first diagnosis and the overall composition, ask where it still feels wrong, and diagnose again from step 2 with that answer.

## Diagnostic prompts

| Feeling | Question |
|---|---|
| Clear but like a template | Where should this product's character show? |
| Polished but not the product I wanted | Does the mood fit the audience and the moment of use? |
| Each part fine, together wrong | Do type, density, spacing, colour and components belong to one voice? |
| The reference looks good, the result is far from it | Was the direction misunderstood, or the chosen direction not executed? |

## Continuity

- Start from the baseline (`decisions.md`). Reuse existing patterns first; a new page follows the accepted direction, and a deviation needs a product reason.
- Add an automated check for a rule that is stable, important and has regressed before. Compare the rest visually, targeted at the page in question.
